# What Readers Love: A Goodreads Data Story

Across 13,525 books and 1.14 million reviews, five patterns stand out about what drives reader engagement on Goodreads — and none of them is simply "more books, more readers."

**Raw popularity concentrates hard at the top.** *The Hunger Games* (5,066,596 ratings) and *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone* (4,972,886) sit more than a million ratings ahead of third-place *Twilight* (3,992,661). The top 15 skews heavily YA/crossover, but decades-old classics like *1984* and *Pride and Prejudice* still hold top-12 spots — a reminder that raw counts reward longevity as much as momentum.

**Catalogue size is a weak predictor of reach.** Across all 5,554 authors, the correlation between number of books published and total ratings earned is just 0.398. J.K. Rowling's 12 books pulled 16,814,800 total ratings; Stephen King's 53 books — more than four times the catalogue — pulled less than half that (7,797,932). What an author writes matters far more than how much.

**Polarizing books skew lower-rated, but not always niche.** Rating variability and average rating are strongly negatively correlated (−0.708): the more readers disagree, the lower the average tends to sit. Most of the top disagreement-generators are lower-profile titles, but *Fifty Shades of Grey* breaks the pattern entirely — a 3.7 average across 1,448,482 ratings proves a book can be simultaneously massive and genuinely divisive.

**Small genres can out-engage large ones.** `non-fiction` (601 books, median 17,359 ratings/book) and `poetry` (182 books, median 15,378) beat every fiction genre on a typical-book basis, while `romance` — the second-largest genre at 9,255 books — has the lowest median (6,095). Category size and per-book engagement pull in opposite directions.

**Highly-rated authors mostly hold their audience, with visible exceptions.** Among the top 8 authors by weighted rating, 7 stay within a tight 4.2–4.7 band across every year in their catalogue. J.K. Rowling is the outlier: her rating drops to 3.3 for *The Casual Vacancy* (2012), her first adult-fiction title, before recovering to 4.2–4.4 with the Cormoran Strike series (2016) — a dip that tracks a genre change, not a general decline.

**Recommendation:** engagement strategy should optimize for per-title quality and audience fit over sheer output. A focused catalogue in a well-matched genre — including under-examined categories like non-fiction and poetry — outperforms volume, and a proven author's rating can survive a genre pivot if the new work still lands with readers.
