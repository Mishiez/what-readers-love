# What Readers Love: A Goodreads Data Story

What drives reader engagement on Goodreads? Across 13,525 books and 1.14 million reviews, the data points to a story that goes beyond simply publishing more books.

**Popularity is highly concentrated.** *The Hunger Games* leads the dataset with 5,066,596 ratings, followed by *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone* with 4,972,886 and *Twilight* with 3,992,661. The top of the list is dominated by YA and crossover fiction, although classics such as *1984* and *Pride and Prejudice* also rank highly.

**Publishing more books does not automatically translate into greater reach.** Across 5,554 authors, the correlation between catalogue size and total ratings is 0.398. J.K. Rowling's 12 books accumulated 16.8 million ratings, while Stephen King's 53 books accumulated 7.8 million. Catalogue size therefore explains only part of the variation in author reach.

**Reader disagreement tends to increase as average ratings fall.** Rating variability and average rating have a strong negative correlation of −0.708. Most highly polarizing books are relatively lower-profile, although *Fifty Shades of Grey* shows that a book can attract both enormous attention and substantial disagreement, with a 3.7 average across 1.45 million ratings.

**Genre size and engagement tell different stories.** Non-fiction and poetry have the highest median ratings per book despite having far fewer books than major fiction categories. This suggests that the size of a genre category does not necessarily reflect the typical level of engagement with each title.

Finally, highly-rated authors in the selected group generally maintain strong ratings across their catalogues, although individual works can diverge. J.K. Rowling's *The Casual Vacancy*, for example, falls to a 3.3 average before later Cormoran Strike novels return to the 4.2–4.4 range.

**Overall, the data suggests that reader engagement is not simply a function of output volume. Popularity is concentrated among a relatively small group of books, catalogue size only partly explains author reach, and per-title engagement varies substantially across genres and individual works.**
